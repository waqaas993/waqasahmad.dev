Mayday Hero: Idle Rescue

An idle emergency-dispatch game where one small-town ambulance grows into a nationwide rescue network. I built it almost single-handedly, from first prototype to store submission.

In testing on Google Play

Overview

Emergency calls arrive on a live map; the player assigns a unit and operator, watches it drive real road routes (or sail water routes for boats), resolves incidents mid-mission, and reinvests funding and reputation into stations, fleet, and crew. Districts, regions, and substations unlock over time, and a prestige layer (“The Charter”) adds a permanent multiplier for long-term play.

What I built

Road-following dispatch, boats on water routes, substations and locked territory, a crew market with XP, tiered ambulances, fire trucks, and helicopters, around 65 authored incident scenarios, world events and surges, a comeback layer, station programs, and a guided first session. On top of that came the commercial layer: rewarded and interstitial ads with consent-first flow, IAP, analytics coverage, Remote Config, and a 14-tab QA debug panel.

Tech stack

React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind, Zustand, Framer Motion, and Leaflet, wrapped for Android and iOS with Capacitor. Firebase Analytics, Crashlytics, and Remote Config; AppLovin MAX and AdMob; AppsFlyer; native IAP. Vitest and Playwright (including visual regression), Storybook, Fastlane, and GitHub Actions for builds.

How I worked

This project is the clearest example of my current workflow: AI-assisted engineering with Claude Code for implementation, an AI art pipeline with a style bible and review step for portraits, vehicles, and scene art, icon A/B variants, and short playtest-driven iteration loops — taking a concept to a store-ready build in roughly three months.
